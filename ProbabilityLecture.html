6. General Multiplication Rule

Used for sequential events, especially dependent events (without replacement).

\[ P(E \text{ and } F) = P(E) \cdot P(F|E) \]

Meaning: Probability of E, times the probability of F given that E already happened.

7. Multiplication Rule for \( n \) Independent Events

Used when multiple events do not affect each other.

\[ P(E_1 \text{ and } E_2 \dots \text{ and } E_n) = P(E_1) \cdot P(E_2) \cdot \dots \cdot P(E_n) \]

This is often used for "all of them" or "none of them" scenarios.

II. Chapter 5 Problem Set

Problem 1 (Addition Rule): In a class of 30 students, 18 play soccer, 12 play basketball, and 5 play both. Find the probability a student plays soccer OR basketball.

Problem 2 (Independence): You roll a 6-sided die and flip a coin. What is the probability of rolling a 4 AND getting tails?

Problem 3 (Conditional): 45% of owners have a dog. 30% have a dog and a fenced yard. Given someone owns a dog, what is the probability they have a fenced yard?

Problem 4 (General Multiplication): You have 7 black pens and 3 red pens. You pick two without replacement. What is the probability of picking a black then a red?

Problem 5 (The "OR" Rule): A card is drawn from a deck. Find the probability it is a King OR a Heart.

Problem 6 (The "AND" Rule): Two balls are drawn from a bag (3 Red, 7 Blue) without replacement. Find \( P(\text{Red then Blue}) \).

Problem 7 (Conditional): 60% of students pass the midterm. 40% pass the midterm and the final. If a student passed the midterm, what is the probability they pass the final?

Problem 8 (The Complement): The probability of a weather sensor failing in extreme cold is 0.07. What is the probability that the sensor does not fail?

Problem 9 ("At Least One"): A backup system has 3 independent power modules. Each has a 10% (0.10) probability of failing. What is the probability that at least one module works?

Hint: Calculate 1 - P(all three fail).
