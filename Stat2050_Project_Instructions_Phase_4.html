STAT 2050 Project: The Class Profile

Instructor & Student Guide

Project Overview & Learning Outcomes

Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs) Assessed:

  • CLO 1: Analyze data with descriptive and numerical statistics (visualizations, measures of central tendency, and variance).
  • CLO 2: Calculate probabilities for discrete and continuous distributions with an understanding of probability concepts and randomness.
  • CLO 3: Analyze data using regression and correlation techniques.
  • CLO 4: Construct hypothesis tests and confidence intervals for population parameters.
  • CLO 5: Construct clear, data-driven statistical arguments from results.

General Instructions:

  • Data Source: See individual phase instructions for which dataset file to use.
  • Submission Format: For written components, you may create your project however you like (Word, Google Docs, etc.), but the final document must be exported and uploaded as a PDF to ensure formatting is preserved. Phase 1 requires two separate submissions: a Google Sheets link and a PDF document.
  • Mathematical Notation: If you struggle to type mathematical symbols or formulas digitally, you may neatly hand-write your equations, take a clear photo, and insert the image directly into your final document.
Academic Integrity & AI Policy:
This project must be your own original work. The use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools (such as ChatGPT, Gemini, etc.) to generate text, analysis, or calculations is strictly prohibited and will be treated as academic dishonesty.

Exception: You may use tools like Grammarly solely for spell-checking and sentence structure improvements. You are also permitted to use AI exclusively for the designated Phase 1 Career Exploration prompt. All Data Analysis must be your own work.

Phase 4: Simulation & Distributions

Focus: Normal models, Binomial models, and Confidence Intervals.

Student Instructions

Part I: Probability Models
Note on Formatting: Ensure that all figures (plots, graphs, etc.) submitted in this phase include clear, descriptive titles.

Step 1: The Normal Model

Access the dataset titled "Simulated Women's Heights based on Spring 2026 data" in our StatCrunch Group. Assume this data follows an approximately normal distribution. Before running calculations, establish your parameters and visualize the distribution:

  • Calculate Parameters: Calculate and report the mean and standard deviation for this simulated dataset.
  • Visualize: Create a histogram of the female heights with a normal curve overlaid atop the histogram. Include this labeled graph in your report.

Use these calculated values as your population parameters (μpopulation mean, mu and σpopulation standard deviation, sigma) to complete the following:

  1. Probability Calculation: Calculate the probability that a randomly selected female student from this distribution is taller than 68 inches. Show your work.
  2. Percentile Calculation: What height represents the 90th percentile for women in this distribution? Show your work.
  3. Personal Percentile: State your actual height in inches (even if you are male). Using this normal model, determine what percentile your height represents. If you use StatCrunch for these calculations, include supporting images of your calculation outputs.

Step 2: The Binomial Model

Use the Two-Way table provided below (which summarizes Math Anxiety Rating vs. Vigorous Exercise) to determine probabilities for a Binomial model. Let a "Success" be a student who answered Yes to "Exercises Vigorously." Imagine you randomly select 12 students from this population, replacing them each time (so trials remain independent).

Two-Way Table: Math Anxiety Rating by Vigorous Exercise
Math Anxiety Rating
Vigorous Exercise 1 2 3 4 Total
No 0 5 3 6 14
Yes 5 20 9 16 50
Total 5 25 12 22 64
  1. Proportions (Success & Failure): Calculate and explicitly report the overall proportion of the class that exercises vigorously based on the table above. We will call this probability of success pp. Also, calculate and report the probability of a student who does not exercise vigorously (q = 1 - pq equals 1 minus p).
  2. Exact Probability: What is the probability that exactly 5 of the 12 students exercise vigorously? Calculate P(X = 5)Probability X equals 5.
  3. Cumulative Probability: What is the probability that at least 2 of the students exercise vigorously? Calculate P(X ≥ 2)Probability X is greater than or equal to 2.
  4. Expected Value: Calculate the expected number of students who exercise vigorously in this sample of size 12 (μ = n × pmu equals n times p). Write a brief sentence interpreting what this expected value means in context.
Part II: Confidence Intervals

Step 3: Estimating the True Proportion

Use our sample data to estimate a population parameter. Let's look at Math Anxiety.

  1. Report Sample Stats: State the sample size (nsample size, n) and calculate the sample proportion (p̂p-hat) of students who reported having High Math Anxiety (Rating 4).
  2. Construct a 95% Confidence Interval: Calculate the 95% confidence interval for the true population proportion of students with high math anxiety. Show your margin of error calculation.
  3. Interpret: Write a standard sentence interpreting your interval in context (e.g., "We are 95% confident that the true proportion of...").

Step 4: Estimating the True Mean

Now, let's look at the continuous numerical variable, Pulse (use your cleaned Phase 1 data).

  1. Report Sample Stats: State the sample size (nsample size, n) and the sample mean (x̄sample mean, x-bar) for the resting pulse data.
  2. Construct a 99% Confidence Interval: Calculate a 99% confidence interval for the true mean resting pulse of the student population. (Note: use a t-distribution since the population standard deviation σsigma is unknown).
  3. Interpret: Write a standard sentence interpreting your interval in context.
  4. Personal Connection: Report your actual resting pulse. Does your pulse fall within the 99% confidence interval you just calculated? Write a brief sentence explaining what this means.

Step 5: Methods Section

Write a brief paragraph explaining the tools used to perform these calculations. Be sure to include any supporting images of your calculations (especially if you used StatCrunch for the Normal Model).
