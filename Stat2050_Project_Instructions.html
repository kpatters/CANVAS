Phase 2: Regression, Leverage, & Probability

Focus: Correlation, outlier analysis, model refinement, and probability rules.

Student Instructions

⚠️ Real Data Alert: Real data is messy. You may calculate an initial correlation coefficient (r) that is close to zero. This is a normal part of the data analysis process before exploring outliers.

Step 1: Initial Scatterplot & Analysis

  1. Variables: For this phase, use Pulse as the Explanatory Variable (x) and Height_Inches as the Response Variable (y).
  2. Visualize: Generate a scatterplot that includes the line of best fit. (Your plot must include a title and labeled axes).
  3. Calculate: Find the correlation coefficient (r), the coefficient of determination (R2), and the regression equation (ŷ = ax + b). Record these values in your report near your scatterplot (StatCrunch generally does not allow you to place these directly on the graph).
  4. Analyze: Based on the visual scatterplot and your r value, briefly explain whether a linear model appears appropriate for this raw data.

Step 2: Identifying & Analyzing Outliers

Look closely at your scatterplot (or refer to boxplots from Phase 1) to identify any unusual data points.

  1. List the Outliers: Create a list of the suspected outliers written as ordered pairs (Pulse, Height).
  2. Evaluate: For each outlier in your list, state whether it has high or low leverage (extreme in the x-direction) and whether it has a large or small residual (extreme in the y-direction).
  3. Conclude: Using these two pieces of information, determine if the point is an influential point (i.e., is it pulling the regression line significantly toward itself?).
💻 Tech Tip: Removing Outliers without Deleting Data
If you need to remove an outlier to see how the model improves, you don't have to delete the entire row! Find the outlier in your dataset and simply add a letter to the numerical value (e.g., change a pulse of 200 to x200). Statistical software will ignore the text entry, dropping it from the scatterplot calculations while keeping your dataset intact.

Step 3: Model Refinement

  1. Refine: Remove the highly influential outlier(s) using the tip above. Note: Once extreme outliers are removed, other hidden outliers may suddenly become apparent. Repeat the removal process until you feel the model is as good as is reasonable.
  2. The Final Model: Generate a new scatterplot without the outliers. Include the new line of best fit, title, and axis labels. Recalculate r, R2, and the regression equation for this new data set.
📋 What to Include in Your Report for Steps 1-3:
  • Your Original Scatterplot and your Refined Scatterplot (both with line of best fit, titles, and labeled axes).
  • The regression equation, r, and R2 listed clearly next to each corresponding scatterplot.
  • Your written Outlier Analysis (list of ordered pairs, leverage, residuals, and influence determination).
  • Your written analysis of whether the original model appeared appropriate, as well as an analysis of whether the linear model seems appropriate for the dataset without the outliers.

Step 4: Interpretation & Predictions (Using the Refined Model)

Note: Proceed with using your refined model to complete the following interpretations and predictions, even if a linear model ultimately may not seem like the best fit for these two variables.
  1. Analyze R2: Write a sentence explaining exactly what your new R2 value tells you about how well the Explanatory variable accounts for the variability in the Response variable.
  2. Contextualize: Interpret the slope and the y-intercept of your refined equation in the context of Pulse and Height. Does the intercept make logical sense?
  3. Standard Prediction: Use your refined equation to predict the height of a student who has a resting pulse of 75 bpm.
  4. Personal Prediction & Residual: Use the equation to predict your own height based on your resting pulse. Calculate your Residual (Actual - Predicted). Finally, interpret your residual in context (e.g., "My residual is negative, so my actual height is lower than the model predicted based on my pulse.")

Step 5: Probability

Create a Two-Way Table (Contingency Table) summarizing the class data using the variable for Vigorous Exercise (Rows) and Math Anxiety (Columns).
Reminder: On the Math Anxiety scale, 1 = No Math Anxiety and 4 = High Math Anxiety.

You must include this contingency table in your report. Then, calculate the following probabilities based on your table:

  • P(High Anxiety): The probability a randomly selected student has High Math Anxiety (rating of 4).
  • P(Exercises Vigorously AND Low Anxiety): Probability of the Intersection (using anxiety rating of 1).
  • P(Does Not Exercise Vigorously OR High Anxiety): Probability of the Union (Addition Rule).
  • P(High Anxiety | Exercises Vigorously): The conditional probability that a student has high anxiety, given that they exercise vigorously.
  • P(Exercises Vigorously | High Anxiety): The conditional probability that a student exercises vigorously, given that they have high math anxiety.
