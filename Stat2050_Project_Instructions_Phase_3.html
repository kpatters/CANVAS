STAT 2050 Project: The Class Profile

Instructor & Student Guide

Project Overview & Learning Outcomes

Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs) Assessed:

  • CLO 1: Analyze data with descriptive and numerical statistics (visualizations, measures of central tendency, and variance).
  • CLO 2: Calculate probabilities for discrete and continuous distributions with an understanding of probability concepts and randomness.
  • CLO 3: Analyze data using regression and correlation techniques.
  • CLO 4: Construct hypothesis tests and confidence intervals for population parameters.
  • CLO 5: Construct clear, data-driven statistical arguments from results.

General Instructions:

  • Data Source: See individual phase instructions for which dataset file to use.
  • Submission Format: For written components, you may create your project however you like (Word, Google Docs, etc.), but the final document must be exported and uploaded as a PDF to ensure formatting is preserved. Phase 1 requires two separate submissions: a Google Sheets link and a PDF document.
  • Mathematical Notation: If you struggle to type mathematical symbols or formulas digitally, you may neatly hand-write your equations, take a clear photo, and insert the image directly into your final document.
Academic Integrity & AI Policy:
This project must be your own original work. The use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools (such as ChatGPT, Gemini, etc.) to generate text, analysis, or calculations is strictly prohibited and will be treated as academic dishonesty.

Exception: You may use tools like Grammarly solely for spell-checking and sentence structure improvements. You are also permitted to use AI exclusively for the designated Phase 1 Career Exploration prompt. All Data Analysis must be your own work.

Phase 3: Correlation & Simple Linear Regression

Focus: Bivariate data, correlation testing, outlier identification, and predictive modeling.

Student Instructions

Data Source Check: Continue using the "Stat2050 Cleaned Master Data" file in StatCrunch for this phase.

Step 1: Scatter Plot & Outlier Identification

We want to examine the relationship between a student's height and their wingspan (arm span). In StatCrunch, construct a scatter plot using Height_Inches as the explanatory variable (xx) and Wingspan_Inches as the response variable (yy).

  • Ensure your scatter plot has a descriptive title and clearly labeled axes with units (inches).
  • Outlier Analysis: Examine your plot carefully. There is one prominent outlier in this dataset. Identify the data point (give its approximate (x, y) coordinates) and write 1-2 sentences commenting on how this outlier affects the visual linear pattern of the data.

Required Question & Formal Answer:

Does a linear relation exist between height and wingspan?
State Yes or No, report your sample size (nn), report your calculated correlation coefficient (rr), and explicitly state whether |r| is greater than the critical value from the table.

Step 3: Simple Linear Regression Model

Run a Simple Linear Regression in StatCrunch with Height as xx and Wingspan as y. Report the following in your document:

  1. Regression Equation: Write the regression equation in the form ŷ = b0 + b1xy-hat equals b naught plus b 1 x (or Predicted Wingspan = Intercept + Slope • Height). Round values to 3 decimal places.
  2. Interpret Slope: Interpret the slope (b1) in the context of height and wingspan (e.g., "For every 1 inch increase in height, wingspan is predicted to...").
  3. Interpret Y-Intercept: Interpret the y-intercept (b0) in context. Explain whether this y-intercept makes practical sense in the real world.
  4. Coefficient of Determination: Report R2R-squared (as a percentage) and interpret what percentage of the variation in wingspan is explained by height.

Step 4: Predictions & Personal Residual

  • Prediction: Use your regression equation to predict the wingspan for a student who is 68 inches tall. Show your substitution work.
  • Personal Residual Calculation: Plug your own actual height into the regression equation to find your predicted wingspan. Then, calculate your personal residual using the formula:
    Residual = Actual Wingspan − Predicted Wingspan Residual equals Actual Wingspan minus Predicted Wingspan
  • Interpretation: Is your actual wingspan above or below what the model predicted for your height? By how many inches?

Step 5: Methods Section

Write a brief paragraph describing the StatCrunch tools and menus used to produce your scatter plot, correlation coefficient, and regression analysis.
