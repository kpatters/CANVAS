STAT 2050 Project: The Class Profile

Instructor & Student Guide

Project Overview & Learning Outcomes

Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs) Assessed:

  • CLO 1: Analyze data with descriptive and numerical statistics (visualizations, measures of central tendency, and variance).
  • CLO 2: Calculate probabilities for discrete and continuous distributions with an understanding of probability concepts and randomness.
  • CLO 3: Analyze data using regression and correlation techniques.
  • CLO 4: Construct hypothesis tests and confidence intervals for population parameters.
  • CLO 5: Construct clear, data-driven statistical arguments from results.

General Instructions:

  • Data Source: See individual phase instructions for which dataset file to use.
  • Submission Format: For written components, you may create your project however you like (Word, Google Docs, etc.), but the final document must be exported and uploaded as a PDF to ensure formatting is preserved. Phase 1 requires two separate submissions: a Google Sheets link and a PDF document.
  • Mathematical Notation: If you struggle to type mathematical symbols or formulas digitally, you may neatly hand-write your equations, take a clear photo, and insert the image directly into your final document.
Academic Integrity & AI Policy:
This project must be your own original work. The use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools (such as ChatGPT, Gemini, etc.) to generate text, analysis, or calculations is strictly prohibited and will be treated as academic dishonesty.

Exception: You may use tools like Grammarly solely for spell-checking and sentence structure improvements. You are also permitted to use AI exclusively for the designated Phase 1 Career Exploration prompt. All Data Analysis must be your own work.

Phase 2: Descriptive Statistics & Visualizations

Focus: Visualizing distributions and calculating measures of center and spread.

Student Instructions

Data Source Check: To ensure everyone is working from the same standard, you will not use your Phase 1 Google Sheet for this section. Instead, open the "Stat2050 Cleaned Master Data" file provided by your instructor in StatCrunch.

Step 1: Organizing the Data

  • Create distribution tables for the Height_Inches variable using StatCrunch.
  • Requirement: The tables must include both Frequency and Relative Frequency.
  • Grouping: You must group this data by Gender (e.g., separate tables or sections for Men and Women) to facilitate comparison.

Step 2: Visualizing the Data

Create the following graphs. Ensure every graph has a clear title and labeled axes.

  1. Histogram: Create a histogram for Height_Inches.
  2. Bar Graph: Create a bar graph for the Math Anxiety Rating. (Note: Pie charts are not accepted for this assignment).
  3. Comparative Boxplots: Create side-by-side boxplots for Height_Inches. Specifically, create one boxplot for males vs. height and one for females vs. height on the same graph.

Step 3: Numerical Summaries & Reflection

Calculate the following statistics for Height_Inches. Create three separate summary tables: one for the full class, one specifically for males, and one specifically for females.

  • Mean (x̄sample mean, x-bar)
  • Median (MMedian)
  • Mode
  • Range
  • Standard Deviation (ssample standard deviation, s)
  • Five-Number Summary: Min, Q1, Median, Q3, Max.

Reflection: After calculating these statistics, write a brief reflection (2-4 sentences) discussing the differences you observe between the male and female summary statistics.

Step 4: The Personal Connection

  • Calculate: Find the z-score for your own height using the FULL CLASS mean and standard deviation.
  • Requirement: You must include the z-score calculation (show the formula with your numbers plugged in) as well as the final result.
  • Interpret: Explain what this z-score tells you. Are you considered "unusual" (more than 2 standard deviations away from the mean)?

Step 5: Methods Section

Write a brief paragraph explaining the tools and menu steps you used in StatCrunch to calculate your numerical summaries and generate your graphs.

📋 Phase 2 Submission Checklist:
  • Frequency and Relative Frequency tables for Height (grouped by gender).
  • Histogram for Height, Bar Graph for Math Anxiety, and Comparative Boxplots for Height by Gender (all titled and labeled).
  • Three summary statistic tables (Full Class, Males, Females) containing Mean, Median, Mode, Range, Standard Deviation, and 5-Number Summary.
  • Reflection paragraph comparing male and female summary statistics.
  • Your personal height z-score calculation and interpretation.
  • Your Methods section paragraph.
