Instructor & Student Guide

Project Overview

  • Data Source: The Stat2050_Survey_Results.csv file (available in Files).
  • Submission Format: Word Document (.docx) or Google Doc.
  • Formatting: Use a readable sans-serif font (e.g., Arial, Calibri) at size 11 or 12.
  • Visuals: Embed all graphs directly into your document.

Phase 1: Data Cleaning & Descriptive Statistics

Focus: Data integrity, visualization, and measures of center. | Due Date: Week 4

Student Instructions

Step 1: Data Cleaning

Open the class data file in Excel or Google Sheets. Correct the following before analysis:

  1. Height Variable: Convert all heights to total inches (e.g., 5'7" becomes 67).
  2. Pulse Variable: Remove any text (like "bpm") so the column contains only numbers.
  3. Categorical Cleanup: Check "Eye Color." If a color has fewer than 2 entries, rename it to "Other."

Step 2: Organizing the Data

  • Create a Frequency Distribution table for the Height_Inches variable. Choose 5 to 7 classes (bins).

Step 3: Visualizing the Data

Create the following graphs. Ensure every graph has a title and labeled axes.

  1. Histogram: Use your frequency distribution to create a histogram for Height_Inches.
  2. Bar Graph or Pareto Chart: Create a bar graph for the Math Anxiety Rating. (Note: Pie charts are not accepted for this assignment).
  3. Comparative Boxplots: Create side-by-side boxplots for Height_Inches separated by Gender.

Step 4: Numerical Summaries

Calculate the following statistics for the full class Height_Inches:

  • Mean (\(\bar{x}\))
  • Median (\(M\))
  • Standard Deviation (\(s\))
  • Five-Number Summary: Min, Q1, Median, Q3, Max.

Step 5: The Personal Connection

  • Calculate: Find the z-score for your own height using the class mean and standard deviation.
  • Interpret: Explain what this z-score tells you. Are you considered "unusual" (more than 2 standard deviations away from the mean)?

Phase 2: Regression & Probability

Focus: Correlation, predictions, and probability rules. | Due Date: Week 8

Student Instructions

⚠️ Real Data Alert: Real data is messy. You may calculate a correlation coefficient ($r$) that is close to zero. This is not necessarily a mistake; it means there may be no strong linear relationship in our sample. Report the math accurately.

Step 1: Regression Analysis

  1. Variables: Use Height_Inches as the Explanatory Variable ($x$) and Pulse as the Response Variable ($y$).
  2. Calculations: Find $r$, $R^2$, and the regression equation \(\hat{y} = ax + b\).
  3. Standard Prediction: Use your equation to predict the pulse of a student who is 68 inches tall.
  4. Personal Prediction: Use your equation to predict your own pulse based on your height. Calculate your Residual (\(\text{Actual} - \text{Predicted}\)).

Step 2: Probability

Create a Two-Way Table for Gender (Rows) and Math Anxiety (Columns). Calculate:

  • P(High Anxiety): Probability a student has High Math Anxiety.
  • P(Man AND Low Anxiety): Probability of the Intersection.
  • P(Woman OR High Anxiety): Probability of the Union (Addition Rule).
  • P(High Anxiety | Woman): Conditional Probability.

Phase 3: Simulation & Distributions

Focus: Randomness, Binomial models, and Confidence Intervals. | Due Date: Week 11

Student Instructions

Step 1: Binomial Simulation

Simulate a random selection of students using a coin toss.

  1. Assume P(Female) = 0.5. Flip a coin 30 times (Heads = Female). Record your number of Heads.
  2. Calculate the Expected Number ($\mu = np$) and Standard Deviation ($\sigma = \sqrt{npq}$).
  3. Is your coin flip result "unusual" (z-score > 2 or < -2)?

Step 2: Confidence Intervals

  1. Proportion: Construct a 95% Confidence Interval for the proportion of students with Blue Eyes. Check conditions: is \(np(1-p) \ge 10\)?
  2. Mean: Construct a 90% Confidence Interval for the mean Commute Time.
  3. Interpretation: Interpret both intervals in the context of the problem.

Phase 4: Hypothesis Testing & Inference

Focus: Formal testing procedures and final synthesis. | Due Date: Week 15

Student Instructions

Step 1: Hypothesis Test (One Sample Mean)

The Claim: Medical guidelines suggest young adults need 8 hours of sleep. Test the claim that Stat 2050 students get less than 8 hours of sleep on average.

  • State $H_0$ and $H_1$. Calculate Test Statistic ($t$) and P-value. Conclusion at $\alpha = 0.05$.

Step 2: Chi-Squared Goodness of Fit

The Claim: Birthdays are evenly distributed throughout the year.

  • Variable: Birth Month. Calculate Expected Count. Run Chi-Square GOF test.
  • Personal Connection: Did your birth month contribute a large amount to the Chi-Square statistic?

Step 3: Executive Summary

  • Physical Characteristics: Discuss height, pulse, and boxplot comparisons.
  • Behavioral Traits: Discuss sleep patterns and anxiety.
  • Statistical Validation: Cite your P-values and Confidence Intervals here.
  • Conclusion: Summarize the "typical" student profile.
